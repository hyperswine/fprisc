# Review of POS v1 (examples/pos1.fpr)

Date: 2026-10-24. Kind: review.
Reviewed revision: main `712f24f34b5d636a6bd50143b4e7e28488d47839`
(merge of `cost-sized-regions`). Scope: `examples/pos1.fpr`, with
`examples/pos1_core.fpr`, `examples/pos1_pricing.fpr`, `std/journal.fpr`,
`std/money.fpr`, the client sync in `std/livejs.fpr` and `deploy/aws-pos1/`.

Method: a reading review. `fpr` was not built and nothing was run; each
finding was traced by hand through the source at the revision above. Line
numbers are into `examples/pos1.fpr` unless a file is named. The findings
are also measured against a separate survey of twenty years of POS failures
(outages, breaches, bad master data, payment flows, lock-in); where a
finding repeats one of those failures it says so.

Status at reviewed revision: no fixes applied. This page records the
findings; fixes, when they land, get their own dated section below or their
own page. Two planning sections follow the findings: a sandboxed path to
real card payments, and what else a real shop needs (authentication and an
Australian legal checklist); their provider and legal details were checked
against the linked sources on 2026-10-24.

## What is already right

The core design avoids most of the failures the survey found:

- **One interpretation of the shop.** `apply` is the only reader of a line,
  for live updates and the boot fold alike, so the state a register shows
  is what a restart rebuilds.
- **A sale is one line in one write.** Receipt, stock change and revenue
  come from that line or none of them do.
- **Corrections are new lines.** A void or restock names what it corrects;
  a committed sale is never rewritten. This is also what Australia's 2018
  ban on electronic sales suppression tools (software that deletes or alters
  sales records) asks of a POS.
- **Disk failures stop the shop rather than lie.** The torn last line is cut
  on open, an unreadable log refuses to start instead of being seeded over,
  and a failed append ends the process (`flush`, l.335) rather than leaving
  a register that answers but never commits.
- **Integer cents and one tax expression** (`std/money.fpr`), shared by the
  browser's cart total and native checkout.
- **Contracts on the arithmetic** in `pos1_core.fpr`: quantity clamping,
  cash change and restock bounds carry refinement types and work budgets.
- **PINs are dropped before the log ships to Loki** (deploy README), and the
  stock metrics file is written beside and renamed over.

The problems below are in the policy around that core, not in the core.

## Findings: fix first

### 1. Every signed-in cashier is an administrator

`withUser` (l.459-469) checks only that the session has a cashier. Any
cashier can therefore void any receipt, `set tax`, `set price`, `retire` a
product, and use `staff NAME PIN` (`staffCmd`, l.557) to reset another
cashier's PIN or create a new login.

Suggested fix: give the `staff` line a role (`cashier` or `manager`), gate
the console, voids, price and tax changes on it, and record a manager
override as its own log line so it appears in the audit trail.

### 2. Login can be brute-forced over the internet

The AWS deploy serves the register on public HTTPS (Caddy on :443). PINs
need only pass `wordy` (l.173), so a one-character PIN is accepted, and
`login` (l.425) has no attempt limit, so a four-digit PIN falls to at most
10,000 tries over one websocket. PINs are also stored in plain text in the
log and in any backup of it.

Suggested fix: per-name and per-connection backoff, a minimum PIN length,
and a salted hash on the `staff` line instead of the PIN.

### 3. Comma decimals are read as huge amounts

`cents` (l.184) skips any character that is not a digit or `.`. "20,50"
(a cashier using a decimal comma) therefore parses as 2050 dollars:
205000 cents tendered, and the change toast offers about $2,044.

Suggested fix: accept only `digits` or `digits.dd` and refuse anything else
with a toast, instead of skipping characters.

### 4. Admin prices are cents, silently, and unbounded

`num` (l.154) reads only leading digits. `set price FW 5.50` (`setV`, l.568)
therefore sets the price to 5 cents; `add SKU 4.50 10 name` (`addCmd`,
l.550) adds a 4-cent product. `add` also accepts a price of 0 and a stock
above the 9999 shelf cap that `Core.restock` enforces, after which the
product can never be restocked (`FullShelf`). `set tax` accepts any number.
This is the Target Canada failure in miniature: unvalidated master data
that flows straight into pricing.

Suggested fix: parse money with the same strict parser as item 3, bound
price, stock and tax at entry, and echo the result in dollars before the
line is committed.

## Findings: wrong for an Australian shop

### 5. Times and "day" are UTC

`fmtTime` (l.202) and `dayOf` (l.219) work in UTC. Receipts show UTC times,
and `sell` (l.502) uses `dayOf` for "first sale of the day", so in Sydney
the celebration fires around 10-11am local time rather than at opening.

Suggested fix: a shop time-zone offset in the settings, applied in both.

### 6. GST is added on top of the shelf price

`totalOf` (l.477) is subtotal plus tax, so the seeded flat white at $5.00
rings up as $5.50. Australian shelf prices are GST-inclusive.

Suggested fix: store the inclusive price, and show GST as 1/11 of the
total (the receipt line can keep recording the tax component).

### 7. Cash totals are not rounded to 5 cents

With 1c and 2c coins withdrawn, `Core.cash` can ask the cashier to give
$4.47 in change.

Suggested fix: round cash totals to the nearest 5c, leave card totals
exact, and record the rounding amount on the sale line so the fold stays
exact.

## Findings: robustness

### 8. A card sale is just a button

`Card` (l.462) records the sale as paid with `pay = 1` and no terminal
involved, so a declined card still becomes a paid receipt.

Suggested fix: split card payment into pending and approved, with the
terminal's approval reference on the sale line. A cashier confirming the
approval by hand is acceptable until a semi-integrated terminal is wired
in; card data should never pass through pos1 (keeps it out of PCI DSS
scope). A sandboxed way to build this is under
[Card payments: a sandboxed path](#card-payments-a-sandboxed-path).

### 9. An acknowledged sale can be lost on power loss

`std/journal.fpr` writes to the open file and replies `Done` without
`fsync`. `update` flushes before the register's reply, so the receipt is
shown only after `write` returns, but the data may still be in the page
cache. On EC2 this is unlikely to matter; on a shop Pi with an unplanned
power cut it can lose sales the cashier has already handed over.

Suggested fix: `fsync`/`fdatasync` in the journal owner before `Done`, or
a `Journal.appendDurable` that pos1 uses.

### 10. Possible double sale after a reconnect

A double-click is safe today: after the first `Cash`, the page's `dirty`
flag is clear (`std/livejs.fpr`, `flush`), no `ClientState` precedes the
second message, and the server's cart is already empty. That safety is
incidental. If the socket drops after a sale is committed but before the
cart-clearing `setClient` (`sold`, l.450) reaches the page, the page
reconnects, `startSync` marks it dirty and mirrors its old cart back, and
the cashier sees a full cart and may ring it up again.

Suggested fix: send the total the page showed and a one-time token with
`Cash` and `Card`; refuse a token already used and a total that does not
match the server's. This also closes the window where a price change lands
between the page's render and checkout.

### 11. Pricing hot-reload has no output check

`pricingAdopted` (l.925) adopts any `pos1_pricing` that type-checks and
binds. A policy whose `rate` returns a negative or 500% tax is accepted.
Routine pushes (a menu refresh, a config change, a content update) caused
most of the outages in the survey.

Suggested fix: before adopting, evaluate the new `rate` on the current tax
and refuse it outside a fixed range (for example 0-5000 basis points),
logging the refusal the way a failed reload already is.

## Architecture

The AWS deploy makes one cloud instance the only copy of the shop: if the
shop's internet drops, every register stops. That is the survey's most
common and most costly failure. The append-only log makes the fix
straightforward: run the source of truth on a store-local box (the Pi
image), and stream log lines to a cloud replica for backup, Grafana and
remote viewing.

## Missing features

- **Day close:** cash count, float, over/short and a Z report. Reporting
  today is all-time revenue only, and that figure includes tax.
- **Receipts:** printed or emailed, including a tax invoice (ABN, GST
  amount) when one is required.
- **Memory over time:** the full sales list stays in the model, and each
  void maps over all of it (`applyVoid`, `markVoid`). Fine for months;
  eventually wants a daily snapshot that the fold starts from.

## Card payments: a sandboxed path

Item 8 asks for real card approval. The low-risk shape is a cloud
("server-driven") terminal: the reader talks to the processor directly, and
pos1 only asks for an amount and is told approved or declined. Card numbers
never reach pos1, and every option below has a test mode that moves no real
money. Provider details were checked on 2026-10-24 and will drift.

### Providers

- **Stripe Terminal (recommended first).** The server-driven integration
  has a built-in simulated reader, so the whole flow can be built with no
  hardware: create a PaymentIntent, tell the reader to process it, then in
  test mode call
  `POST /v1/test_helpers/terminal/readers/{id}/present_payment_method` to
  stand in for the customer's tap. Simulated test cards produce declines
  (generic, insufficient funds, lost, stolen, expired, processing error),
  PIN prompts, and include Australian eftpos cards. Later, a real reader
  plus Stripe's physical test card works only against the sandbox, and the
  cents of the amount choose the outcome (`.00` approves, `.05` declines,
  `.55` wrong PIN). Terminal is available in Australia in AUD; check the
  Dashboard's reader shop for which models are sold locally.
  [Testing](https://docs.stripe.com/terminal/references/testing),
  [availability](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-terminal-global-availability).
- **Square Terminal API.** The orders integration is available in
  Australia. The sandbox takes special device IDs that succeed, have the
  buyer cancel, time out, or behave as an offline terminal, which is a
  good test of failure handling. Successful sandbox checkouts are limited
  to small amounts (under US$25).
  [Sandbox](https://developer.squareup.com/docs/devtools/sandbox/testing),
  [availability](https://developer.squareup.com/blog/announcing-mobile-payments-sdk-ga-and-new-terminal-api-features/).
- **Tyro.** Australian and strong on eftpos. Its test integration server
  uses simulated card schemes, but needs a specially configured terminal
  and an API key from Tyro's integration team, so it is a later option.
  [Test server](https://docs.integrated-eftpos.tyro.com/integrated-eftpos/tta/testintegrationserver).

`std/httpclient` already speaks HTTPS over native TLS, so pos1 can call any
of these REST APIs itself.

### Fitting it to the log

The dangerous moment is a crash or dropped link while a charge is in
flight, so that moment goes in the log like everything else:

1. **`cardstart`** is committed before the charge is requested. It carries
   the receipt number and an idempotency key derived from it, so a retried
   request can never charge the same receipt twice.
2. **`sale … card ref=<payment id>`** on approval, or **`cardfail <reason>`**
   on decline, cancel or timeout. Stock and revenue still come only from
   the `sale` line.
3. **Boot reconciles.** Any `cardstart` without a result is looked up with
   the processor by its key before registers are served, so "did we charge
   them?" is a lookup, not a guess.
4. **The call runs in its own actor** (a `Live` command) and its answer
   comes back as a message, so the model stays the only writer of the log.
   The register shows "waiting for card" meanwhile.
5. **Live money needs an explicit act.** The secret key lives in an
   environment variable on the server only, restricted to terminal
   payments. pos1 refuses to start with a live key (`sk_live_`) unless
   given `--live`, and caps live amounts at first. With no network, card is
   unavailable and the page says cash only.

### Order

1. A fake processor inside fpr with the same interface, which randomly
   approves, declines, stalls or times out, so `pos1-check.py` can drive the
   crash and race cases offline.
2. Stripe's simulated reader in test mode.
3. A real reader with the physical test card.
4. Live, with an amount cap.

## Before real use: auth and legal

There is no licence or certification for POS software in Australia. What
makes pos1 fit for a real shop is that its records and receipts meet tax
law and that card handling stays with the processor. This section is a
checklist to take to the shop's accountant, not legal advice.

### Authentication

Items 1 and 2 are best fixed with keys where they fit, and PINs where
speed matters:

- **Enrolled devices.** Each register (tablet, Pi screen) holds its own key
  pair, approved once by the owner. The server refuses any unenrolled
  device before the login screen is served, which removes the brute-force
  problem: a stranger cannot reach the PIN prompt.
- **Cashiers** keep a short PIN, typed only on an enrolled device. Staff at
  a counter need to sign in within a second.
- **Owner and manager** use passkeys (WebAuthn) for the console, voids
  above a limit, and remote access.
- **Accounts** are created by the owner, with a role; there is no public
  sign-up, and every account change is a log line.

### A single shop

- **Tax invoices.** For a sale over $82.50 including GST, the customer can
  ask for a tax invoice and must get it within 28 days. It needs: that it
  is a tax invoice, the seller's identity, the ABN, the date, the items
  with quantities and prices, the GST amount (or a statement that the total
  includes GST), and how much of each sale is taxable. A receipt that
  carries all of these can serve.
  [ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/gst-excise-and-indirect-taxes/gst/tax-invoices).
  pos1 produces no receipt today.
- **GST-inclusive prices and 5c cash rounding:** items 6 and 7.
- **Records kept for five years and never altered.** The log has the right
  shape; it must never gain a delete or edit tool (the 2018 ban on sales
  suppression tools). Missing: automatic off-site backups, and item 9.
- **Refunds.** Consumer law entitles customers to refunds for major
  faults. A void does not refund last week's sale: add a `refund` line
  that names the original receipt, with partial amounts.
- **Card payments through a processor,** as above. The shop then completes
  a short annual PCI self-assessment with its payment provider, simplest
  when card data never touches pos1.
- **Privacy.** Businesses under $3M turnover are generally exempt from the
  Privacy Act today. The 2026 exposure draft of the next reforms does not
  address that exemption, so it is worth watching.
  [Draft](https://www.hsfkramer.com/fr/insights/2026-09/the-draft-tranche-2-privacy-act-reforms-whats-there-whats-new-and-whats-missing).
  If pos1 collects customer emails for receipts, a short privacy notice is
  good practice either way.
- **An end-of-day export** the shop's accountant can import.

### Selling or hosting it for other businesses

All of the above, plus terms of service, liability limits and data-handling
commitments; backups and uptime that can actually be delivered; support
during trading hours; and a plan for security incidents. Consumer law also
covers business purchases under $100,000, so "it broke" claims apply.

### Realistic first deployment

One shop the builder knows: a store-local box (see Architecture), Stripe
Terminal, receipts carrying that shop's ABN, and backups. An hour with the
shop's accountant before going live, to confirm the receipts and the
end-of-day export meet their needs.

## Suggested order

Items 1-7 are small and local to `pos1.fpr` and `pos1_core.fpr`; the parsers
in 3-4 belong in `pos1_core` with contracts and oracle cases in
`tests/check_pos_core.py`. Items 8-11 touch `std/journal` or `std/live` and
the page protocol. The architecture change is a deploy and replication
project of its own. Card payments follow their own four steps above, and
the auth items replace the fixes suggested for 1 and 2. Receipts, refunds
and backups are what stand between the fixed register and a first real
shop.
