# Performance profile of the application workloads (x64, 14 November)

Kind: measurement record and proposal. Applies to `main` at 3485b92f. Its `compiler/`, `runtime/`, `std/`,
`core/` and `machine/` trees are identical to 23f10023. Raw data is in [perf/2026-11-14-profile/](perf/2026-11-14-profile/).

Every figure marked **measured** comes from a run on this box. Figures marked **est.** are estimates
derived from the instruction and time shares; they were not measured.

## Setup and method

* x64 native, Debian trixie, an 8-vCPU VM. **The VM exposes no hardware performance counters** (perf reports
  `cycles` and `instructions` as not supported). Time shares therefore come from **perf `cpu-clock` sampling**
  (20 kHz, which includes kernel time), and exact instruction counts come from **valgrind callgrind** 3.24
  (`--dump-instr=yes`). Each executed instruction was joined with `objdump -d` and classified.
  Page faults were recorded one by one (`perf record -e page-faults -c 1 -d`).
* Timing uses one warm-up, then shuffled, interleaved rounds. A run starts only when the 1-minute load
  average is ≤ 1.5. Results are the median wall time and the median peak RSS (from `wait4`), and every output was checked
  against `tests/bench/apps/expected/`. The baseline uses 5 rounds; the experiments behind a decision use 7–10.
* Inputs come from `gen.py`, recovered from `1b4aca16^`.
* **Peers: only C (`cc -O2`), Rust (`-C opt-level=3`) and Go were available.** The ratios below are against
  C or against the C/Go/Rust median. They are **not comparable** with the earlier five-peer figure (3.2x
  the median, which included OCaml and Java).

## Corrections to what we said before

* **sim is 0.41 s / 14 MB today**, not 1.37 s / 160 MB. The earlier figure could not be reproduced on this box.
* **String-slice views are at most about 5%.** substr plus its allocation is about 7% of csv time and 3–5% of wordfreq.
  The pieces are 1–5 bytes, so a view cell would be no smaller than the copy. The string-side cost
  lies elsewhere: in the scan loops, and in csv's Int parsing.
* **Reuse and regions now cost time; they are not a missing win.** Reuse works: the sim loop rewrites its cells in
  place, and treemap rebuilds its map in place. Loop regions and copy-out are needed for memory: without
  them csv goes from 170 to 336 MB and wordfreq from 341 MB to 1.79 GB. Their bookkeeping, though, costs 5–11% of time,
  and in an in-place loop copy-out is pure overhead.
* **x64 codegen is the largest bucket, but not because of cross-call register allocation or bump spills.** The
  hot loops make no calls and do not allocate. What costs is per-instruction selection (see sim below).

### Findings by app

* **sim:** 99% of instructions are in one loop, at **166 instructions per particle-step against C's 18**.
  The loop makes no calls and does not allocate. Its instruction mix, as a share of all instructions:
  * unconditional `jmp` 11.9%
  * tag/untag/overflow sequences 10.8%
  * comparisons materialized to 0/1 and then tested 9.5%
  * frame spill/reload 8.3%
  * `movabs` of small constants 6.0%
  * register moves 14.3%
  * fuel ticks 1.8%

  Two builds that execute identical instructions (6.714 G vs 6.711 G) ran at 0.404 s and 0.355 s over 10 rounds.
  Their unaligned hot loop heads sit at different addresses.
* **csv:**
  * The `charFrom` scan takes **28 instructions per byte** over about 30.6 M bytes: two compare/`set`/`movzbq`/`test`
    sequences, three `jmp`s, an untag of the invariant byte, a dead re-tag of the index, a `movabs $1`, and a
    4-instruction fuel tick in TLS memory.
  * Int parsing takes about 528 instructions per number.
  * The map walk makes 9.16 M string compares.
  * Kernel time is 25.5%. 82.9 k faults touch 324 MB to reach a 170 MB peak. 38% of the faults come from the `memset` of copy-out
    targets, and about a quarter from the stack growth of `String.lines`.
* **wordfreq / wordfreq_mutmap:**
  * With `std/map`, the AVL walk plus string compares take about 56% of time (24.9 M compares).
  * In mutmap, `insertWith` is 32% of instructions. The known combine closure goes through `fpr_apply`, with an
    allocation, a `memcpy` and a free on every call (13.5% of instructions). `mm_str_hash` hashes byte by byte (6.8%).
* **treemap:** 80% of time is in AVL walks, at about 4,400 instructions per insert, and it is also latency-bound.
  `std/hashmap` (an AVL tree ordered by hash, not a hash table) is slower than `std/map` for three reasons:
  * its final `toMap` sort calls the comparator through the generic closure path (`g_sortListWith → apply2 → fpr_applyN → callf`, 7.2 M calls, 29% of instructions)
  * the insert walk costs 15% more instructions
  * the hash is called through a closure.

### The wordfreq_mutmap memory regression (231 → 341 MB)

* aee9ce39 (12 November) rewrote `String.words` as front-to-back cons recursion. On x64 it does not
  become a loop: `fpr_fn_wordsGo` calls itself, which builds about 2 M nested native frames. Faults on the tops of fresh stack pages
  account for 46.9 k faults, 183 MB touched.
* **Measured:**
  * The same source built by the 57201f0 and b8518da compilers peaks at 231 MB.
  * On `main`, an accumulator `words` gives 231 MB; wordfreq_mutmap goes from 0.713 to 0.655 s (−8%) and its sys time from 0.194 to 0.136 s.
  * An accumulator `lines` takes csv from 170 to 144 MB.
* `FPR_NO_CALL_REGIONS=1` does not change it, and why the loop conversion misses `wordsGo` is still open.

## Suggested fixes, in order

Each gain is a share of that app's wall time today. The gains overlap, so they do not add up.

1. **`String.words`/`String.lines` back to bounded stack.** Either restore an accumulator plus reverse, or make the loop
   (TRMC) conversion catch `wordsGo`/`linesGo`.
   **Measured:** wordfreq and wordfreq_mutmap −110 MB peak RSS (341 → 231 MB); wordfreq_mutmap −8% wall;
   csv −26 MB.
2. **Stop the immediate page release on Linux.** `hal_heap_release` (`machine/unix/host.c`) calls
   `MADV_DONTNEED` on every freed buddy block, so the pages are faulted in again later; macOS already uses `MADV_FREE`.
   Use `MADV_FREE`, or retain blocks with some hysteresis.
   **Measured**, with release disabled: csv faults drop from 82.9 k to 44.3 k and wall time from 0.711 to 0.627 s (**−12%**,
   7 rounds) at +4 MB RSS. No change for wordfreq.
3. **Tight byte-scan loops.** Make `charFrom` a memchr-style scan, make `isSpace` a range or table test, and tick
   once per loop instead of once per byte.
   **est.** csv −12–17%, wordfreq_mutmap −15–20%, wordfreq −8–11%. The basis: scans are 13–25% of time,
   at 28 instructions per byte where about 4 (or memchr's fraction of 1) would do.
4. **x64 instruction selection.**
   * Fuse compare and branch instead of materializing 0/1, and thread jumps to the next block.
   * Use immediates instead of `movabs`.
   * Drop redundant re-tags and loop-invariant untags.
   * Keep loop values in registers instead of frame slots.
   * Align hot loop heads.

   The removable-instruction classes put an upper bound on the gain: sim 37%, treemap 30%, csv/wordfreq 23%, mutmap 17%
   of all instructions. **est.** sim −25–35%, treemap −20–25%, csv and wordfreq −15–20%, mutmap −10–15%.
   Loop alignment alone looks worth about 10% on sim (est., from one pair of builds).
5. **Follow-ups:**
   * **Lighter copy-out in in-place loops.** Skip the copy-out walk where the loop rebuilds its state in place (owned
     maps), and back off after declines. **Measured** with copy-out off: treemap −16% wall (1.164 → 0.976 s,
     10 rounds) and 60 → 43 MB. With call regions off, csv was −3% (10 rounds) and 6.2% fewer instructions (est. 5–10% from
     skipping call regions around callees that never take a slab). Loop regions and copy-out themselves must stay.
   * **Direct calls for known closures** in `MutMap.insertWith`/`alter` and in `sortWith`: **est.** wordfreq_mutmap
     −12–18% together with a word-at-a-time string hash; the closure path is 29% of treemap_hashmap's instructions.
   * **A real hash table behind `std/hashmap`** instead of an AVL tree ordered by hash. For wordfreq with an ordered map,
     the remaining half of the gap is the 24.9 M string compares, so this choice, not codegen, decides it.

Lower priority: parse Int from a string range (est. csv −5–8%); string-slice views (est. ≤5%).
