# Typed IR, milestone M3: liveness, allocation, frames and the investment review

Date: 2026-10-21. Kind: implementation record and the investment review
[the plan](2026-10-20-TYPED-NATIVE-IR-PLAN.md) requires at the end of M3,
on the branch `typed-ir-m3` (cut from `codex/a64-overflow-checks-20261020`
at `34c3ff9`, the M2 state). The runtime contracts are the
[M0 register](2026-10-21-NATIVE-IR-CONTRACTS.md)'s; the selected-instruction
vocabulary, the policy and the A64 and x86-64 selectors are
[M2's](2026-10-21-TYPED-IR-M2-A64.md) and
[M2's second half's](2026-10-21-TYPED-IR-M2-X64.md); the budget per target
is the [register-allocation design](2026-10-19-REGISTER-ALLOCATION.md)'s
section 3.3. Audience: compiler implementers and reviewers.

This change replaces M2's conservative placement (every virtual value in a
frame slot) with live ranges and a linear-scan allocator in the opt-in
typed-IR backend, native prologues and epilogues that save only the
callee-saved registers a function uses, shared spill slots, and the self
tail call as a loop. Nothing changes a default: the backend is still behind
`FPR_TYPED_IR_BACKEND`, and the legacy emission with it off is untouched
(the generator's `codegenRev` is unchanged; the selection revisions are
bumped, below).

## Switches

| Variable | Values | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `FPR_TYPED_IR_BACKEND`, `FPR_TYPED_IR_STRICT`, `FPR_TYPED_IR` | as in M2 | unchanged |
| `FPR_TYPED_IR_SELF_LOOPS` | `0` | the native backends keep a self tail call as the legacy re-entry through the function's symbol; unset or anything else: the loop form below |

The loop switch is in the unit-cache identity (`-noloops` after the backend
tag) and in the run-cache key. The selection revisions are now A64 3 and
x86-64 2 (`a64SelectionRev`, `x64SelectionRev`): any cached unit from M2 is
invalidated, which is what the revision is for.

### Liveness

The selected stream is not SSA: a block parameter is written by every
predecessor's parallel copy and a loop-carried one again at the loop's
tail. Liveness is the classic backward dataflow over the stream's basic
blocks (a label starts one, a transfer ends one, a conditional transfer's
fall-through is a successor), with `instrLiveSets` giving the exact live-in
and live-out set of every instruction over any integer-keyed register
notion (virtual registers here, physical ones in the oracle).

Each virtual register is then summarised as one interval on a position
line: instruction *i* sits at 2*i*; a value live into a block whose first
instruction is at 2*f* starts at 2*f*-1; a value live out of a block whose
last instruction is at 2*q* ends at 2*q*+1. So an interval that starts at
an even position starts with a definition by that instruction, and one
that ends at an even position ends with a use by that instruction, and
those are the only two points where two intervals may meet in one
register. The intervals come from the block-level sets, never by
enumerating the per-instruction sets (whose total size is the overlap of
every range): a 4200-value function (the frame-ceiling fixture) is
analysed in well under a second.

The interval ignores holes (a value defined on both sides of a slow path
is one interval over the slow path's call), which is conservative for
register occupancy. The constraints are exact: whether a value crosses an
instruction (live in and out, not defined there), is born there or dies
there is read from the per-instruction sets, and only at the instructions
that reserve a physical register, grouped by reservation set with an early
exit. That exactness is what lets a call's argument die in a caller-saved
register and a value defined on both sides of a slow path avoid the
callee-saved budget.

### Allocation

The scan is Poletto and Sarkar's: intervals in start order, the first free
allowed register, otherwise the active interval with the fewest accesses
(definitions plus uses, what a spill costs in stores and reloads) is
evicted to a slot when it has fewer than the new one, else the new one is
spilled. A spilled value lives in its slot for its whole range. There is
no interval splitting, no use-density weighting beyond the access count,
and no coalescing beyond two cases: a tied definition takes its use's
register when the use dies there, and a value born in a fixed register or
dying into one prefers that register (the hint that puts a call's result
straight into x19 or a return value straight into x0).

The allowed registers of a value are the target's two pools minus what the
instructions inside its range reserve:

- across an instruction (live in and out): everything it names, so a value
  across a call (whose clobbers are every caller-saved register) can only
  take the callee-saved budget: x19..x27 on A64 (what `ctx_a64.S` saves,
  never x28), rbx and r12..r15 on x86-64 (what `ctx_x64.S` saves). This is
  the contract: nothing but the saved frame pointer, the stack pointer and
  the hart register lives in a register across a safepoint outside the
  scheduler-preserved set. No float register is ever allocated: an `FPR`
  operand is refused by name; M3 has no float values, and the register
  says the context switch saves no float registers, so a float pool would
  have to be caller-saved only, a later milestone's decision;
- born there: the instruction's implicit definitions and the fixed
  registers of its other definitions (its clobbers happen before the
  definition exists: a call's result is copied out afterwards), plus what
  it reads when the expansion may write early (x86-64's `divide`: `cqto`
  writes rdx before `idivq` reads the divisor);
- dying there: its implicit uses (the fixed registers of its other
  operands are met by the parallel copy before it, its clobbers and
  definitions happen after the reads), plus what it writes when the
  expansion may write early.

A value that crosses no call prefers the caller-saved pool (free) to the
budget (a save and a restore per activation). The pools: A64 callee-saved
x19..x27, caller-saved x9..x12 then x8..x0 (the argument registers are
allocatable: a value dying into x0 as an argument costs nothing), scratch
x13..x15 (never allocated: reloads of spilled operands, dead definitions,
copy cycles); x86-64 callee-saved rbx, r12..r15, caller-saved rax, rcx,
rdx, rsi, rdi, r8, r9, scratch r10 and r11 (the expansions' own scratch
inside the entry sequence and the epilogue, free between instructions).

Per instruction the rewrite emits: a parallel copy before it (fixed use
registers from the values' locations, reloads of spilled unconstrained
uses into scratch, the tied use into the destination register), the
instruction over physical registers, and a parallel copy after it (fixed
definitions into their locations, spilled definitions stored from scratch).
`resolveCopies` emits stores first (their sources are read before any
register is written), then register destinations in an order where a
destination is not a pending source; a cycle (the staging of a call that
permutes its arguments, `rot3`) is broken through a scratch register that
is neither a source nor a destination. A selected move whose two sides end
in one register is dropped. A return or tail transfer that defines a value
is refused, as in M2.

### Spill slots and frames

Spilled values are assigned slots in start order; per kind (size,
alignment, managed status, never an escaping object) the free slot released
earliest is taken first when its last occupant's range ended before the
new one begins, else a new slot is created. The frame is computed
afterwards: the link area, the saved registers (one word each, adjacent),
then the slots, 16-aligned, the legacy shape. The 32768-byte ceiling is
unchanged and still refused by name; the fixture that exercises it now
keeps 4200 values live at once.

### Prologues and epilogues

A64: `enter` as in M2, then the saves as `stp` pairs at signed scaled
offsets from x29 (two adjacent words whose lower offset is a multiple of 8
within [-512, 504]; a register without a partner or beyond that range is a
single `stur`/`str`; x27 is never paired with x28, which is not in the
budget), then the arrival copies (`mov x19, x0`, or a store when the
parameter spilled; a parameter never used costs nothing), then the entry
sequence, whose stack check and fuel tick are byte for byte M2's. Before
every `ret` and every tail transfer the restores (`ldp`, x29-relative, so
after a stack-segment change they still read the frame they were saved
in) precede the legacy epilogue. x86-64: the same with `movq` saves and
restores, and the seventh and eighth parameters read from the private
cells into registers (`load.cell`) before the entry sequence can destroy
them, as M2's `park.cell` did into slots.

### The loop form

A tail transfer to the function's own symbol with as many fixed arguments
as parameters becomes a parallel copy into the parameters (a cycle broken
with a fresh virtual register, as the selectors' block-argument moves do)
and a jump to a label placed after the entry block's stack check and
before its tick: the entry pseudo is split into `entry.check` and
`entry.tick`, each keeping its implicit definitions, clobbers and calls.
The frame is constant, so the check runs once; the tick runs once per
iteration, as the re-entry did, so the bound analysis sees the back edge
cross a safepoint. The parameters are then live around the loop and take
the budget, saved once per activation instead of once per iteration. This
is the legacy `FPR_SELF_LOOPS` form in the new backend, behind
`FPR_TYPED_IR_SELF_LOOPS=0` for the separate measurement below. On x86-64
the seventh and eighth arguments are staged by the selector into the
private cells just before the transfer; the loop form takes those stores
back as the arguments they carry (the arrival names the cells), so a
seven- or eight-parameter loop runs without touching the cells.

### The oracle

`checkAllocation` runs on the physical stream of every function: it
recomputes liveness over physical registers and refuses a stream in which a
clobbered register is live out of its clobbering instruction, a register
outside the preserved set is live across an instruction with a call, a
float register is live across one, or a register is read before the
arrival or any instruction wrote it. It knows nothing of the allocator's
bookkeeping; a defect in the scan, the rewrite or the copy resolver that
leaves a value in a wrong register across a call fails the compile.

### Bounds

`native-insns-v1` is recomputed over the final stream as before: the
saves, the arrival copies, the reloads, the stores, the staging copies and
the restores are all rendered instructions and count. The loop form's
back edge is bounded through `entry.tick`.

### The header line and the notes

Every native function's header now reads

```
# fn f (arity a) typed-ir a64 frame=N saved=x19,x20 values=V regs=R spills=S slots=K reloads=L stores=T copies=C crossing=X loops=Y
```

and the backend note (`typed-ir-backend: f native (frame N saved=... ,
native-insns-v1 ...)`) carries the same facts, which the tests read.

### Compile time: the constant 60 to 70 ms

Every build with the backend on took 0.06 s longer than without
(0.54 to 0.60 s on the four small roots, 3.65 to 3.71 s on sha): above the
10 % trigger on the small roots, so investigated. Compiling alone is not
slower: `fpr --target=a64mac --prelude=core/prelude.fpr tests/bench/fib.fpr`
takes 0.14 s with and without `FPR_TYPED_IR_BACKEND=mixed` (five runs each,
on the mini). The difference is in `fpr build`'s assembly of the cached
prelude unit, which is kept as text and assembled on every build: the
mixed-mode prelude unit is 630574 bytes against 547537 (267 functions
either way, 113 of them native), and `cc -c` of it takes 0.36 s against
0.31 s. The native functions' text is longer (every local label carries
the mangled function name, every return carries its restores, every
arithmetic operation its slow path), not necessarily their code: the
`__TEXT` segment grows by one 16 KiB page. Caching unit objects rather
than unit text, or shorter local labels, would remove it; neither is M3's.
Peak compiler memory is unchanged (88 to 90 MiB on the small roots, 143
to 145 MiB on sha, within the run-to-run spread).

### Paired timings: the investment review

`tools/bench-typed-ir-m3.py`, on the M1 Mac mini (above; it carries about
two cores of background load from the services it hosts, which the
paired, alternating design tolerates but which widens the intervals),
driver commit `5990e28`, compiler `4916e8e3d3bb`: five variants, every
binary built before any timing, one warm-up each, 31 rounds in an order
that rotates by one each round and reverses every other block, every
output checked. Fib, sum and pipeline read an internal monotonic clock
around the kernel; helpers and SHA are process wall time with startup.
`median of paired ratios` is the median over rounds of (variant time /
baseline time) in the same round; the interval is a seeded bootstrap of
that median (2000 resamples, 95 %); `faster pairs` counts the rounds in
which the variant was faster. Raw samples, hashes, flags and build facts:
[`bench2-results.json`](evidence/2026-10-21-typed-ir-m3/bench2-results.json),
the tables as the driver printed them:
[`bench2-summary.md`](evidence/2026-10-21-typed-ir-m3/bench2-summary.md)
(a first run with four variants,
[`bench-summary.md`](evidence/2026-10-21-typed-ir-m3/bench-summary.md),
agrees within the intervals). Variants: `legacy` (the defaults),
`legacy-best` (`FPR_COSTED_REGISTERS=1 FPR_COMPACT_FRAMES=1
FPR_SELF_LOOPS=1 FPR_A64_FRAME_PAIRS=1`), `typed-ir`
(`FPR_TYPED_IR_BACKEND=mixed`), `typed-ir-noloop` (that with
`FPR_TYPED_IR_SELF_LOOPS=0`), `typed-ir-best` (the backend for the
functions it covers, the best legacy combination for the rest: the fair
comparison against `legacy-best`, since the functions the scalar lowering
refuses stay legacy either way and their opt-ins are an unrelated setting).

**Investigations** (the plan's triggers: a median slowdown above 2 %, a
compile-time or memory increase above 10 %):

- *sha, 3.4 % slower than the best legacy combination, 6.8 % against the
  plain variant.* The root has no native function; the slowdown is in the
  imported units' native functions. Under `typed-ir-best` those are, in
  the units sha links, mostly one- and two-parameter wrappers around a
  foreign call (`std/string`'s `append`, `compare`, `contains`, `fromInt`,
  `join`, `left`, the file and socket wrappers), which the legacy
  generator emits as shallow leaves *without* a stack check (29 functions
  of one unit, none with a check; natively all 29 have one) and without a
  saved register, while every native entry runs the check (M2's
  `StackCheckEveryEntry` policy) and a parameter that merely crosses the
  entry tick takes x19, saved and restored (`saved=x19 values=1` in the
  notes). That is eight to twelve instructions and a branch per call of a
  wrapper that does almost nothing else. The fix is the legacy shallow
  rule in the policy (`StackCheckLegacyShallow` is declared and refused
  since M2) and letting a parameter that is only passed on ride its
  argument register through the tick, neither of which is in this change.
  Against the legacy default sha is unchanged (1.0000), so the native
  wrappers are no worse than the default's.
- *Compile time, +0.06 s per build (11 % on the small roots, 1.6 % on
  sha).* Investigated above: the assembly of the larger cached prelude
  unit text, not the compiler; peak memory unchanged.

the best validated legacy opt-in combination on fib, sum and helpers by at

Code size: the typed-IR binaries are 17 to 18 KiB larger (one 16 KiB
`__TEXT` page on the small roots), from the mixed prelude unit.
Maintenance: the change is about 1500 lines (`Liveness.hs` 200,
`Allocate.hs` 480, the two targets' configuration, prologues and
epilogues 250, the tests 450, the driver 220), replacing none of the
legacy generator yet; the legacy path's four opt-in switches remain the
best combination for everything the backend does not cover.

**Decision.** Two of the three speed goals are not met, and one
no-regression goal is not met, so by the plan's own trigger M4 does not
follow automatically from this page. The assessment: where the allocator
operates on the shapes it was built for (a self-recursive loop, a
recursive worker) it reaches parity with four legacy opt-ins at once,
with a third of their frame bytes and bounds and no spills; where the
legacy generator's slot traffic dominates (call-heavy small helpers) it
is 28 % faster, which no legacy switch achieved; where it loses, the
cause is identified and small (the per-entry stack check and a parameter
parked across the tick) and lies in the policy, not the allocator; and
the largest gains are gated on coverage (helpers' loop, SHA's digest), the
sibling work that widens the scalar lowering. The recommendation is to
proceed to M4 after two bounded, separately switched and measured steps
on this backend: the shallow stack-check rule in the policy, and passing
a parameter that is only forwarded without parking it; and to re-run this
driver on the M4 before any claim about that host. This page does not
change a default and does not promote the backend.

## What was not done, and what is next

- The allocator is the initial one the plan asked for: one interval per
  value (holes count as live for occupancy), no splitting, the spill
  choice by static access count, coalescing only through the tied rule
  and the fixed-register hint. A loop-carried value evicted under pressure
  is spilled for its whole range. Interval splitting and a use-density
  weight are the next allocator steps if M4 proceeds.
- No float register is allocated; no float value exists in the scalar
  subset. The register's rule (the context switch saves no float
  registers) makes any float pool caller-saved only.
- The entry stack check still runs in every function (M2's policy); the
  loop form skips it per iteration, as the legacy loop form does, but a
  shallow leaf still pays it once.
- The scalar lowering's coverage is M2's: helpers (`band`), SHA and the
  pipeline's vector kernels do not reach the backend, so the review below
  measures them for parity only. Widening the lowering is the sibling
  coverage work, not this change.
- The x86-64 backend's allocation is executed under Rosetta with the
  substituted TLS and runtime of the local harness, and by `tests/SelectedIR.hs`;
  the native Linux leg (`tests/check_typed_ir_x64.py` in CI) was not run
  here, as M2's gate still records.
- Not verified: anything on a board, under QOS, on Linux; the `all`
  strict mode on a corpus; energy.
